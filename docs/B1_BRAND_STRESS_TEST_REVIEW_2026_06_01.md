## Visual Decision

The slide uses the hybrid SpaceBio-Bench identity rather than the earlier
generic bridge style:

- warm/cool scientific canvas rather than decorative space wallpaper;
- one large recessed evidence field rather than card boxes;
- scattered source-study marks on the left;
- a thin alignment rail in the center;
- a raised task surface and score ledger on the right;
- one dominant transfer path from fragmented public studies to evaluation.

Reference calibration:

- primary: `kmu_proof_stage`;
- secondary: `pbs_benchmark_concept`, `isgp_live_rhythm`.

This is the right calibration mix for B1 because the slide needs to explain a
method concept to a first-time viewer while also opening the live deck spine.

## Text And Jargon Review

Visible text is 36 words, below the 45-word bridge-slide budget.

Visible terms:

- `Public space omics needs an evaluation layer.`
- `SpaceBio-Bench organizes public studies into traceable tasks and audited scores.`
- `Public studies`
- `Missions, tissues, samples, labels`
- `Benchmark tasks`
- `Audited scores`
- `public-data resource; source-traceable`
- `Source: project inventories and source records.`

Jargon assessment:

- `Public space omics`, `public studies`, `tasks`, and `scores` are acceptable
  for the target audience and are supported by the visual structure.
- `Missions, tissues, samples, labels` is domain language, but it is concrete
  enough for a first-time viewer because it sits on the alignment rail.
- `source-traceable` is the only term likely to need a spoken bridge. In the
  speaker note, define it plainly as: every task can be followed back to source
  records and project inventories.
- No internal terms appear in visible text.

Forbidden visible-term scan:

- pass: no `payload`, `RRRM`, `alpha`, `LOMO`, `macro-F1`, raw local path,
  package name, or planning language appears in the rendered slide.
